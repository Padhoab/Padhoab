1. Basic Structure Doctrine

Relevant Article(s): 368

Meaning: Parliament cannot destroy the basic structure of the Constitution.

Landmark Case(s): Kesavananda Bharati (1973), Indira Gandhi (1975), Minerva Mills (1980), Kihoto Hollohan (1992), I.R. Coelho (2007)

2. Doctrine of Eclipse

Relevant Article(s): 13(1)

Meaning: Pre-Constitution laws inconsistent with Fundamental Rights remain dormant and may revive.

Landmark Case(s): Bhikaji Narain (1955)

3. Doctrine of Severability

Relevant Article(s): 13

Meaning: Only the unconstitutional portion is struck down.

Landmark Case(s): R.M.D. Chamarbaugwala (1957)

4. Doctrine of Waiver

Relevant Article(s): 14–32

Meaning: Fundamental Rights cannot be waived.

Landmark Case(s): Basheshar Nath (1959)

5. Doctrine of Colourable Legislation

Relevant Article(s): 246

Meaning: Indirect exercise of prohibited legislative power is invalid.

Landmark Case(s): K.C. Gajapati (1953)

6. Doctrine of Pith and Substance

Relevant Article(s): 246

Meaning: Validity depends on the true nature of legislation.

Landmark Case(s): Prafulla Kumar (1947), F.N. Balsara (1951)

7. Doctrine of Repugnancy

Relevant Article(s): 254

Meaning: Central law prevails over conflicting State law.

Landmark Case(s): M. Karunanidhi (1979)

8. Doctrine of Prospective Overruling

Relevant Article(s): 141

Meaning: Judgment applies prospectively.

Landmark Case(s): Golaknath (1967)

9. Doctrine of Harmonious Construction

Relevant Article(s): -

Meaning: Conflicting provisions should be interpreted harmoniously.

Landmark Case(s): C.P. & Berar Act Case (1939)

10. Doctrine of Public Trust

Relevant Article(s): 21

Meaning: State is trustee of natural resources.

Landmark Case(s): M.C. Mehta v. Kamal Nath (1997)
